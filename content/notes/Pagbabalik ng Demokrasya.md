# Edsa Revolution

## 1st Day of ESA Revolution
---
[Day 1 Explainer](https://www.tiktok.com/@mightymagulang/video/7338377167954332933?_r=1&_t=ZS-96d1wXO7a1V)
- Planned EDSA feature portrayed the conflict as:
    - “forces of good vs. forces of evil”
    - similar to a _Star Wars_-style battle.
- Confusion surrounded the exact sequence of events.
- Reports disagreed on timing of:
    - Jaime Cardinal Sin’s Radio Veritas appeal
    - Butz Aquino’s public call.
- Some accounts placed Cardinal Sin’s appeal around 9 PM.
- Other reports claimed:
    - Butz Aquino spoke first
    - or called after 10 PM.
- Information spread unevenly and inconsistently through media.

---
## 2nd Day of EDSA Revolution
---
[Day 2 Explainer](https://www.tiktok.com/@mightymagulang/video/7338853434164301061?_r=1&_t=ZS-96d1tPICMFx)
- Different newspapers and reports gave conflicting versions of events.
- Multiple press conferences by:
    - Ferdinand Marcos
    - were often confused with one another.
- Public reaction initially mocked Marcos’s claim of a coup attempt.
- Juan Ponce Enrile denied the accusation and called it:
    - “a bunch of bull.”
- Later revelations suggested:
    - Marcos’s coup claims were true
    - Enrile used psychological warfare.
- Raised uncertainty over:
    - which reports during EDSA were factual
    - and which were propaganda.

---

## 3rd Day of EDSA Revolution
---
[Day 3 Explainer](https://www.tiktok.com/@mightymagulang/video/7339119558327913734?_r=1&_t=ZS-96d1zyrJmM9)
- Military and political figures began recounting their experiences of EDSA.
- Key individuals involved included:
    - Fidel V. Ramos
    - Rene Cruz
    - Jose Almonte
    - Avelino “Sonny” Razon.
- Accounts from these figures helped reconstruct:
    - military actions
    - internal decisions
    - developments inside Camp Aguinaldo.
- Radio broadcasts from:
    - Ramos
    - June Keithley
    - became important records of the revolution.

---

## 4th Day of EDSA Revolution
---
[Day 4 Explainer](https://www.tiktok.com/@mightymagulang/video/7339542789996301573?_r=1&_t=ZS-96d22cy1YbU)
- Additional testimonies and perspectives emerged from:
    - civilians
    - military officials
    - public figures.
- Accounts included stories from:
    - Freddie Aguilar
    - Rose Marie Arenas.
- Corazon C. Aquino later shared her recollections of the revolution.
- Debate continued over foreign involvement.
- Irene Marcos-Araneta argued:
    - EDSA was not purely Filipino because of American assistance.
- Author concluded:
    - Americans largely remained on the sidelines
    - and were also surprised by the rapid developments.
- Events of EDSA showed:
    - rapid spread of information
    - conflicting narratives
    - weakened reliability of journalism after years of censorship during Martial Law.
